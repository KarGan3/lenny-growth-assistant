# Defining Activation Milestones for B2B Onboarding Success [1]

As a B2B product manager, one of the key challenges is to effectively measure and track user activation. According to Lauryn Isford, who led the activation team at Airtable, a clear definition of activation milestones is crucial for growth teams. [1] Isford emphasizes the importance of having a singular focus on what the business results are that the team is working to drive. [1]

At Airtable, the activation team focused on a specific metric: what they called their team activation metric. [1] This metric was determined to be highly correlated with long-term retention. [1] It required a number of things to be true, including that something of substance had been built, people persisted and continued to collaborate, and they were collaborating on a weekly cadence. ## Step 2: Understanding Activation Milestones for B2B Onboarding [2]

According to Williams, activation is about forming a habit around the usage of a product, specifically deriving core value by fixing vulnerabilities. [2] This definition is crucial because security is a team effort, and teams may consist of multiple people with different roles. [2]

For Snyk, activation is defined as fixing vulnerabilities within 30 days of team creation. [2] This timeframe is chosen because there is a significant correlation with downstream metrics, such as three-month retention and retention rates. [2]

### Suggested Application

To apply this understanding to your B2B product, consider the following:

* Identify the core value that your product provides to users, such as fixing vulnerabilities or achieving a specific business goal.
* Define activation milestones that align with this core value, such as completing a specific task or achieving a certain level of engagement.
* Establish a correlation between activation milestones and downstream metrics, such as retention rates or customer satisfaction.
* Use this definition to track user progress and provide personalized feedback and support.
* Consider using a habit-forming approach to encourage users to continue using your product over time.

## Step 3: Focusing on Existing User Retention for B2B Onboarding Success [3]

According to Cheng, companies often focus most of their energy on existing user retention mechanics, as this lever has a significant impact on daily habit formation and compound retention. [3]

Grammarly's success demonstrates the critical role of the activation installation "aha moment" in carrying users for a long time. [3] This suggests that a product's ability to create a strong initial connection with users is essential for long-term retention. [3]

### Suggested Application

To apply Cheng's insights to your B2B onboarding strategy, consider the following:

* **Conduct a thorough analysis of your existing user retention metrics**: Understand which users are most likely to become long-term customers and what factors contribute to their retention.

* **Identify the "aha moment" in your onboarding process**: Determine the specific moment when users are most likely to form a strong connection with your product and focus on optimizing this experience.

* **Prioritize cost-efficient and efficient onboarding processes**: Ensure that your onboarding process is lean and effective, allowing you to survive on the margin of retention to growth costs.

## Step 5: Designing a User-Centric Onboarding Experience [5]

Merci Grace's advice on onboarding emphasizes the importance of considering the user's experience from the very beginning. [5]

**Suggested Application**

* Conduct user research to understand the user's motivations and pain points, and use this information to inform the onboarding experience.
* Design the onboarding process to be intuitive and engaging, with clear instructions and minimal friction.
* Use a user-centric approach to framing the onboarding process, focusing on the user's goals and objectives rather than the product's features.
* Test and iterate on the onboarding experience to ensure that it is effective and efficient.
* Consider using a "first day" or "first week" experience to set the user up for success and provide a positive foundation for future engagement.
