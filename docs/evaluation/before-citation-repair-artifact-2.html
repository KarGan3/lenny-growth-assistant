Defining Activation Milestones for B2B Onboarding Success

Introduction

As a B2B product manager, defining activation milestones is crucial for growth teams. A clear definition of activation milestones helps focus efforts on what the business results are that the team is working to drive. [1]

Establishing a Clear Activation Metric

Lauryn Isford, Head of Growth at Airtable, emphasizes the importance of having a singular focus on what the business results are that the growth team is working to drive. [1] In Airtable's case, the activation metric was determined to be highly correlated with long-term retention. [1] This metric required a number of things to be true, including substance being built, people persisting and collaborating, and holding a high bar on exactly how they're contributing. [1]

1. Establish a clear activation metric that is highly correlated with long-term retention. [2]

2. Define the metric in terms of specific behaviors or outcomes, such as week four, multi-user, or a certain level of collaboration. [1]

3. Ensure the metric holds a high bar, requiring multiple conditions to be met. [1]

Focusing on Team Activation

Ben Williams, VP of Product at Snyk, emphasizes the importance of focusing on team activation. [2] For Snyk, activation is indicative of the team forming a habit around the usage of the product, specifically fixing vulnerabilities. [2]

3. Emphasize the importance of security as a team sport. [2]

Prioritizing Existing User Retention

1. Prioritize existing user retention, especially for products with daily frequency. [3]

3. Use retention metrics to inform product development and growth strategies. [2]

Scrappy Hiring and Growth

For Gojek, growth was set up as a core product team, with a focus on filling gaps and solving problems. [4]

1. Prioritize scrappy hiring and growth, especially for products with rapid growth. [4]

Conclusion

By establishing a clear activation metric, focusing on team activation, prioritizing existing user retention, and prioritizing scrappy hiring and growth, teams can drive growth and retention. [2] Remember to step out of yourself and recognize people have different motivations and are in different stages of the journey. [5]
