# Defining Activation Milestones for Improved User Engagement [1]

As a growth practitioner, you're likely familiar with the challenge of measuring user activation. However, defining a clear activation milestone can be a daunting task. According to Lauryn Isford, a key metric to focus on is what constitutes an activated user. At Airtable, the team identified a specific milestone: a representative team of users was considered activated when they had built something of substance, persisted, and collaborated on a weekly cadence. This metric was found to be highly correlated with long-term retention [1]. By setting a high bar for activation, the team aimed to achieve a more meaningful and sustainable level of engagement.

## Step 1: Establishing a Clear Activation Metric for Focus and Direction [1]

To improve user activation, it's essential to define a clear activation metric that serves as a north star for the growth team. This metric should be highly correlated with long-term retention and hold a relatively high bar, requiring substance and persistence from users. According to Lauryn Isford, a key metric at Airtable was the activation milestone of a team of people being activated on the product and using it in a way that suggested long-term retention.

This approach helps to focus the growth team's efforts on driving meaningful user behavior, rather than chasing arbitrary metrics. By establishing a clear activation metric, teams can set high standards for themselves and work towards achieving them for as many customers as possible.

### Practical Steps to Establish an Activation Metric

* Identify key behaviors that indicate long-term retention and collaboration among users
* Determine a metric that holds a relatively high bar, requiring substance and persistence from users
* Correlate the metric with long-term retention data to ensure its effectiveness
* Establish a clear and measurable definition of the activation milestone
* Communicate the activation metric clearly to the growth team and stakeholders

## Step 2: Define Activation Milestones Based on Core Habits [2]

To improve user activation, it's essential to define activation milestones that align with core habits. According to Ben Williams, activation is indicative of a team forming a habit around Snyk usage, specifically fixing vulnerabilities. This definition is crucial because security is a team sport, and different team members may fulfill different parts of the activation journey.

Defining activation milestones based on core habits helps to focus on meaningful engagement rather than just logging in. By setting milestones related to fixing vulnerabilities, teams can measure their progress and retention over time. This approach also acknowledges that teams may not always be actively engaged, but still contribute to the overall security posture.

**Practical Steps to Define Activation Milestones:**

* Identify the core habits that align with your product's purpose (e.g., fixing vulnerabilities)
* Establish a clear definition of activation milestones that reflect these core habits
* Consider the team's role and responsibilities in the activation journey
* Set milestones that measure progress and retention over time
* Regularly review and adjust the definition of activation milestones to ensure alignment with your product's goals and user needs.

## Step 3: Focusing on Existing User Retention for Daily Habit Formation [3]

When it comes to improving user activation, it's essential to understand the importance of existing user retention. According to Albert Cheng, a key factor in determining a product's success is its ability to create a habitual pattern with its users. This means that the product's retention rate is crucial in building a daily habit, which compounds and drives user engagement over time [3].

For products with daily frequency, such as those with daily habits, existing user retention is the most critical factor. However, this approach may not be suitable for all products. For instance, Grammarly's success highlights the importance of the activation installation aha moment, which can carry users for a long time. This suggests that a product's initial experience and onboarding process can have a significant impact on user retention.

To focus on existing user retention, consider the following practical steps:

* Analyze your product's retention rate and identify areas for improvement
* Develop strategies to increase user engagement and create a habitual pattern
* Invest in cost-efficient and efficient operations to survive on the margin of retention to growth
* Monitor and adjust your retention-focused efforts regularly to ensure optimal results

## Step 4: Embedding Growth into Core Product Teams for Improved Activation [4]

Crystal Widjaja's experience at Gojek highlights the importance of integrating growth into core product teams to address user activation challenges. By embedding growth professionals into these teams, the company was able to fill gaps in the product and improve user experience.

This approach is effective because it allows growth professionals to work closely with product managers to identify and prioritize user needs. By synthesizing growth objectives with product features, the company was able to create a more cohesive and user-centric product.

To implement this approach, consider the following steps:

* Identify key growth objectives and align them with product features
* Embed growth professionals into core product teams to facilitate collaboration and knowledge-sharing
* Prioritize user needs and feedback to inform product development
* Monitor and evaluate the impact of growth objectives on user activation and engagement

## Step 5: Designing for the Onboarding Experience: A Key to Unlocking User Activation [5]

When designing an onboarding experience, it's essential to consider the user's journey from the very beginning. This involves thinking about how the user will discover the product, what steps they'll need to take to integrate with it, and how the product will feel native and deeply tied into their experience.

According to Merci Grace, designing the onboarding experience from the start can make a significant difference in user activation. She emphasizes the importance of considering the user's motivations and stages of the journey, and being willing to adapt and adjust the onboarding process accordingly.

Here are some practical steps to design an effective onboarding experience:

* **Start with the end goal**: Think about what you want the user to achieve or experience after completing the onboarding process.

* **Consider the user's motivations**: What drives the user to engage with your product? What are their pain points or challenges?

* **Design for the first interaction**: How will the user discover your product? What steps will they need to take to get started?

* **Make it intuitive and seamless**: Ensure that the onboarding process feels natural and effortless, with minimal friction or confusion.

## Takeaway [5]

To effectively design an onboarding experience that unlocks user activation, it's essential to consider the user's journey from the very beginning. This involves thinking about how the user will discover the product, what steps they'll need to take to integrate it into their workflow, and how the product will be presented to them. By doing so, you can create a seamless and intuitive experience that sets the user up for success. According to Merci Grace, designing the onboarding experience from the outset can make a significant difference in how users engage with the product. [5]
