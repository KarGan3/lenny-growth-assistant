## Step 1: Establishing a Clear Activation Metric for B2B Onboarding [1]

To effectively measure user activation, it's essential to define a clear and meaningful activation metric. According to Lauryn Isford, a key aspect of this is to determine what constitutes an "activated user" and what milestone marks the transition from onboarding to long-term retention [1]. This requires a deep understanding of the product's goals and the behaviors that indicate a user's commitment to using it.

A well-defined activation metric serves as a north star for the growth team, providing a singular focus on what drives business results. In the case of Airtable, the team's activation metric was tied to a specific milestone: week four, multi-user, where more than one person on a team is active and contributing to a workflow [1]. This metric requires a high bar, indicating that something of substance has been built, and that users persist and collaborate on a regular cadence.

**Suggested Application**

* Identify the key behaviors that indicate user activation, such as completing a specific workflow or achieving a certain level of engagement.

* Determine a clear milestone that marks the transition from onboarding to long-term retention.

* Establish a robust analytics framework to track and measure the activation metric.

## Step 2: Developing a Habit Moment Around Core Usage

To set a habit around core usage for B2B activation, it's essential to focus on the team's ability to derive core value from the product. This means measuring the team's progress in fixing vulnerabilities, rather than just logging in or finding vulnerabilities. By doing so, you can establish a clear activation milestone that indicates the team has formed a habit around the usage of the product.

The key is to identify moments that mark a significant shift in the team's behavior, such as fixing vulnerabilities within a specific timeframe. This helps to enumerate aggregate-level activity and provides a more comprehensive understanding of the team's engagement.

**Suggested Application:**

* Set a specific timeframe for teams to fix vulnerabilities, such as 30 days, to establish a habit moment around core usage.
* Focus on measuring the team's progress in fixing vulnerabilities, rather than just logging in or finding vulnerabilities.
* Establish a clear definition of activation milestones that indicate the team has formed a habit around the usage of the product.
* Use this definition to track team engagement and identify areas for improvement.

## Step 3: Focusing on Existing User Retention for B2B Activation [3]

When it comes to improving user activation in B2B onboarding, it's essential to consider the importance of existing user retention. According to Albert Cheng, a key factor in retaining users is making the product "sticky" and creating a habitual pattern [3]. This means that the product should be designed to encourage users to continue using it over time.

By focusing on existing user retention, companies can create a daily habit that compounds and builds user engagement. This approach is particularly effective for products with daily frequency, such as those that require daily usage. In contrast, products with less frequent usage, like Grammarly, may require a different approach, with a focus on the activation installation "aha moment" [3].

Suggested application:

* Conduct user research to understand the habits and patterns of your target audience
* Design your product to create a sense of familiarity and consistency
* Prioritize features and functionality that encourage daily usage and engagement
* Monitor and analyze user retention rates to identify areas for improvement

## Step 4: Integrating Growth into Core Product Teams for Enhanced User Activation [4]

Crystal Widjaja's experience at Gojek highlights the importance of integrating growth into core product teams to address user activation challenges. By embedding growth professionals into these teams, they were able to fill critical gaps in the product's onboarding and adoption processes.

[4] At Gojek, growth was initially focused on addressing small acquisition, adoption, and engagement use cases, such as providing clear instructions for new users to find drivers and complete their first ride. This approach helped to improve the overall user experience and increase engagement.

### Suggested Application

To apply this principle to your own B2B product, consider the following steps:

* Identify key areas where growth can fill gaps in the product's onboarding and adoption processes
* Embed growth professionals into core product teams to provide expertise and guidance on user activation challenges
* Collaborate with growth teams to develop clear instructions and protocols for new users to complete critical tasks and achieve key milestones
* Monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of these efforts to ensure that growth is having a positive impact on user activation and overall product success

## Step 5: Designing for the Onboarding Experience [5]

When designing an onboarding experience, it's essential to consider how the user will discover and interact with the product. This involves thinking about the steps between the user and the full value of the app, and how to frame the introduction in a way that matters to the user.

According to Merci Grace, designing the onboarding experience from the beginning can be a crucial aspect of creating a successful product. She emphasizes the importance of considering how the user will be introduced to the product, what steps they will need to take, and how the product will integrate with their existing workflows.

Suggested application:

* Conduct user research to understand how users will interact with the product and identify pain points in the onboarding process
* Develop a clear and concise onboarding flow that guides the user through the necessary steps to achieve the full value of the app
* Consider the user's existing workflows and integrate the product in a way that feels native and deeply tied into the user experience

## Takeaway [5]

To effectively design an onboarding experience, consider the first introduction to your product as a critical moment in the user's journey. Think about how you will introduce your product to the user, what steps you will ask them to take, and how you will frame the value of your app. This mindset can help you create a seamless and engaging onboarding process.

Suggested application:
* Conduct user research to understand how users will interact with your product during onboarding
* Design the onboarding experience to be intuitive and easy to follow
* Avoid using generic, plug-and-play frameworks that may not be tailored to your specific product
* Test and iterate on the onboarding experience to ensure it meets your product's unique needs and goals [5].
