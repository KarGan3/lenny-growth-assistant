# Defining Activation Milestones for B2B Onboarding [1]

As a B2B product manager, defining an effective activation milestone is crucial for driving long-term retention and business results. According to Lauryn Isford, former growth team lead at Airtable, every growth team needs a north star metric to focus on what drives business results. [1] For Airtable's activation team, the north star metric was a team activation metric, which measured the activation of a representative team using the product in a way that suggested long-term retention. [1]

This metric required a high bar, as it involved multiple users collaborating on a workflow within a specific timeframe. [1]

The activation metric was highly correlated with long-term retention, indicating that it was a strong indicator of a user's commitment to the product. [1]

## Step 1: Establishing a Clear Activation Metric for B2B Onboarding [1]

Lauryn Isford, from Airtable, emphasizes the importance of having a singular focus on what the business results are that the growth team is working to drive. [1] In Airtable's case, the team activation metric was a key metric to determine when a user was considered "activated" [1]. This metric was based on a user's behavior on the platform, specifically the ability to collaborate with others on a weekly cadence. [1]

* Identify the key behaviors or milestones that indicate a user is engaged and active on your platform. [1]
* Determine the specific criteria that must be met for a user to be considered "activated". [1]
* Establish a clear and measurable way to track and monitor this metric. [1]
* Use data analytics to determine the correlation between this metric and long-term retention. [1]

Suggested application:

* Establish a clear timeline for achieving these milestones, such as a specific number of days or weeks.

* Use this metric to inform your onboarding strategy and identify areas for improvement.

### Put the lesson into practice

Lauryn Isford from Airtable emphasizes the importance of having a clear understanding of what it means for a user to be "activated" on a platform. [1] In Airtable's case, the team activation metric was based on a user's ability to collaborate with others on a weekly cadence. [1] This milestone was considered a key indicator of engagement and activity on the platform. [1]

To apply this insight to your own B2B product, consider establishing a clear timeline for achieving these milestones.

Suggested application:

* Set a specific number of days or weeks for a user to complete a key task or achieve a specific behavior on your platform.
* Use this timeline to inform your onboarding strategy and identify areas for improvement.
* Consider using data analytics to track user progress and adjust your onboarding strategy accordingly.

## Step 2: Setting a Clear Activation Milestone for B2B Onboarding [2]

Ben Williams from Snyk emphasizes the importance of defining activation milestones based on team habits, rather than individual user behavior. [2] According to Ben, activation is indicative of a team forming a habit around the usage of a product, specifically deriving core value by fixing vulnerabilities. [2] This definition is crucial because security is a team sport, and teams often consist of multiple people with different roles. [2]

Ben's approach to activation milestones involves setting moments, such as aha moments and habit moments, which are related to fixing vulnerabilities within a specific timeframe. [2] For Snyk, this timeframe is 30 days after team creation, as there is a significant correlation with downstream metrics like three-month retention. [2]

### Suggested Application

To apply Ben's approach to your B2B product, consider the following steps:

* Identify the key habits or behaviors that indicate successful onboarding for your team-based product.
* Set a specific timeframe for these habits to emerge, such as 30 days after onboarding.
* Establish a clear definition of activation milestones based on these habits, rather than individual user behavior.
* Use this definition to track team-level engagement and retention, and adjust your onboarding strategy accordingly.

## Step 3: Focusing on Existing User Retention for B2B Onboarding [3]

According to Cheng, companies often focus most of their energy on retaining their existing user base, as this retention rate has a compounding effect on building daily habits [3].

### Suggested Application

To apply this principle to your B2B onboarding strategy, consider the following steps:

* **Monitor and analyze existing user retention rates**: Track the retention rates of your existing users to understand how well your onboarding process is working.
* **Identify areas for improvement**: Analyze the data to identify areas where users are dropping off and make adjustments to your onboarding process accordingly.
* **Prioritize retention over acquisition**: Focus on retaining your existing users, rather than solely relying on acquiring new ones.
* **Test and iterate**: Continuously test and refine your onboarding process to optimize user retention and daily engagement.

## Suggested Application for B2B Product Managers

Crystal Widjaja from Gojek highlights the importance of growth teams filling gaps in the product, particularly in areas like first-time user experience. [4] By embedding growth into core product teams, companies can ensure that key aspects of onboarding are addressed.

The key takeaway from Crystal's experience is that growth teams can help identify and prioritize areas for improvement, such as providing clear instructions or guidance for new users. This approach allows growth teams to work closely with product managers to ensure that the product is meeting the needs of both existing and new users.

### Suggested Application

* Identify areas of the onboarding process where new users may be struggling or feeling unclear about how to use the product.
* Work with product managers to prioritize and address these areas, ensuring that the product is meeting the needs of both existing and new users.
* Embed growth teams into core product teams to provide a unified approach to onboarding and user experience.
* Regularly review and assess the effectiveness of these efforts to ensure that the product is meeting its goals and providing a positive user experience.

## Step 5: Designing for a Seamless Onboarding Experience [5]

This approach involves considering the user's journey from the outset, taking into account how users will discover the product, what steps they will need to take to integrate with the product, and what value they will gain from using it. [5]

### Suggested Application

To apply this approach to your B2B product, consider the following steps:
