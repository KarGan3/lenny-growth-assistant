# Activation Guidance for B2B Onboarding
=====================================

### Defining Activation Milestones

According to Lauryn Isford, former growth team lead at Airtable, every growth team needs a north star metric to focus on what drives business results. [1] For Airtable's activation team, the north star metric was a team activation metric, which measured the activation of a representative team using the product in a way that suggested long-term retention. [1]

This metric required a high bar, as it involved multiple users collaborating on a workflow within a specific timeframe. [1]

### Setting Activation Milestones

Ben Williams, VP of Product at Snyk, emphasizes the importance of setting clear activation milestones. [2] For Snyk, activation is indicative of the team forming a habit around the usage of the product, specifically fixing vulnerabilities. [2]

Ben recommends focusing on team-level activity, as security is a team sport. [2] This approach helps to enumerate aggregate-level activity around fixes, which is highly correlated with long-term retention. [2]

### Prioritizing Existing User Retention

 For products with daily frequency, retention is the most critical metric, as it compounds and builds daily habits. [3]

However, for products like Grammarly, which require installation and setup, the activation installation "aha moment" is critical for long-term retention. [3]

### Onboarding as a Growth Lever

 Onboarding can be one of the biggest levers for retention, and it's essential to approach onboarding as a growth team. [1]

Merci recommends designing the onboarding experience from the user's perspective, considering how to introduce the product and frame the value proposition. [5]

### Practical Recommendations

1. **Define a clear activation milestone**: Set a high bar for activation, such as a team activation metric that measures the activation of a representative team using the product in a way that suggests long-term retention.
2. **Focus on team-level activity**: Prioritize team-level activity, as security is a team sport.
3. **Prioritize existing user retention**: Focus on existing user retention, especially for products with daily frequency.
4. **Design the onboarding experience**: Design the onboarding experience from the user's perspective, considering how to introduce the product and frame the value proposition.
5. **Measure and optimize**: Measure the effectiveness of your onboarding strategy and optimize it based on user feedback and data.

By following these guidelines, your team can create an effective activation strategy that drives long-term retention and business results.
