# The Struggle is Real: Understanding User Activation for B2B Product Managers [1]

As a B2B product manager, you're likely no stranger to the challenges of getting your product off the ground. One of the most significant hurdles is user activation – the process of turning interested users into loyal customers. But what exactly is user activation, and how can you measure its success? According to Lauryn Isford, a growth expert at Airtable, a clear definition of activation is crucial for setting a north star metric that drives business results. [1]

## Step 1: Developing a Clear North Star Metric for Activation [1]

Lauryn Isford, a growth expert at Airtable, emphasizes the importance of having a singular focus for activation milestones. This north star metric serves as a guiding light for the growth team, helping them prioritize efforts and measure progress. By establishing a clear activation metric, teams can ensure that their efforts are aligned with the business results they aim to drive.

A well-defined activation metric requires careful consideration of the key elements that contribute to long-term retention. According to Isford, Airtable's activation metric involves a team of people being activated on the product and using it in a way that suggests they will be long-term retained. This metric is highly correlated with long-term retention and holds a relatively high bar, requiring substance, persistence, and collaboration.

To develop a clear north star metric for activation, consider the following steps:

* Identify the key elements that contribute to long-term retention
* Determine a metric that holds a relatively high bar, but is still achievable
* Establish a clear definition of the activation milestone
* Ensure that the metric is aligned with the business results you aim to drive
* Regularly review and refine the metric to ensure it remains effective

## Step 2: Setting a Clear Activation Milestone for B2B Product Managers [2]

To effectively improve user activation, it's essential to define a clear activation milestone that aligns with the company's goals and expectations. According to Ben Williams, activation is indicative of a team forming a habit around the usage of Snyk, specifically fixing vulnerabilities [2]. This definition is crucial because security is a team sport, and different people may fulfill different parts of the team activation journey.

Defining an activation milestone helps to enumerate aggregate level activity around fixing vulnerabilities, which can be measured at a team level rather than individual user level. By setting a specific timeframe, such as 30 days, teams that fix vulnerabilities within this period are more likely to experience downstream benefits, including improved retention.

Here are some practical steps to set a clear activation milestone:

* **Define activation in terms of behavior**: Identify the specific actions or behaviors that indicate activation, such as fixing vulnerabilities or deriving core value.

* **Align with company goals**: Ensure the activation milestone aligns with the company's security goals and expectations.

* **Set a specific timeframe**: Establish a clear timeframe for activation, such as 30 days, to provide a measurable benchmark.

## Step 3: Balancing Workspace and User Activation Metrics for B2B Product Managers [3]

Lauryn Isford's experience emphasizes the need for a balanced approach to evaluating user activation in B2B product management. By considering both workspace and user-specific metrics, teams can gain a more complete understanding of their users' engagement.

To achieve this balance, product managers can focus on analyzing user behavior and assessing workspace creation. This involves tracking user-specific metrics, such as login frequency or feature usage, to understand individual user engagement. At the same time, monitoring workspace creation, such as board creation, can provide insight into team-level engagement and collaboration.

By evaluating both perspectives, product managers can set a hybrid metric that balances workspace and user-specific metrics. This approach allows for a more comprehensive understanding of user activation and enables data-driven decisions to improve product engagement and retention.

## Step 4: Focusing on Existing User Retention: A Key to Unlocking User Activation [4]

When it comes to improving user activation, B2B product managers often focus on acquiring new users. However, this approach can be misguided, as it neglects the importance of retaining existing users. According to Albert Cheng, a successful growth model focuses on existing user retention, as it is a much bigger lever for growth [4]. This is particularly true for products with daily frequency, where retention is the key to building a daily habit.

For products like Grammarly, which require installation and daily use, the activation installation aha moment is critical for long-term retention [4]. This highlights the importance of understanding the unique needs and pain points of each product and adjusting the focus accordingly.

To apply this insight to your own product, consider the following steps:

* **Conduct a user retention analysis**: Identify the existing user retention rate and understand the factors that contribute to it.
* **Develop a retention-focused growth strategy**: Allocate more resources to retention efforts, such as improving the onboarding process, reducing friction, and increasing engagement.
* **Monitor and adjust**: Continuously monitor user retention metrics and adjust the strategy as needed to optimize retention and activation.

## Step 5: Focusing on Existing User Retention: A Key to Unlocking User Activation [5]

As B2B product managers, we're often focused on acquiring new users, but neglecting the importance of retaining existing ones. However, as Albert Cheng and Lenny Rachitsky highlight, the key to unlocking user activation lies in understanding the importance of existing user retention.

According to Cheng, successful consumer subscription products start "very scrappy and very cost-efficient" because they're constantly trying to find what works and survive on the margin of retention to growth cost [5]. This approach is crucial for B2B product managers, who often have limited resources and need to make the most of their existing user base.

Rachitsky's experience with his newsletter is also telling. He notes that it's a "difficult treadmill" to retain users, as people are constantly looking for ways to save money and spend on other things [5]. However, by focusing on finding more people coming than going, he's able to create a sustainable business model.

So, what can B2B product managers learn from Cheng and Rachitsky? Here are some practical steps to improve user activation:

* **Analyze your existing user base**: Understand the demographics, behavior, and retention patterns of your existing users. This will help you identify areas for improvement and create targeted strategies to retain them.
