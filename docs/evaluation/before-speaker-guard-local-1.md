# Defining Activation Milestones for B2B Products [1]

As a B2B product manager, you're likely struggling to define what it means for a user to be "activated" on your platform. This is a crucial metric, as it determines the effectiveness of your onboarding process and long-term retention strategies. At Airtable, the activation team focused on a single north star metric: what they called their team activation metric. This metric was determined to be highly correlated with long-term retention and required a number of things to be true, including substance built, persistence, and collaboration on a weekly cadence.

• Identify a specific, measurable milestone that indicates a user is engaged with your platform.

## Step 1: Establishing a Clear Activation Metric for B2B Products [1]

In Airtable's case, the team activation metric was a key metric that determined when a user was considered "activated" [1]. This metric was based on a user's behavior on the platform, specifically the ability to collaborate with others on a weekly cadence.

## Step 2: Defining Activation Milestones for B2B Products [2]

Ben Williams from Snyk emphasizes the importance of defining activation milestones for teams, rather than individual users. According to Ben, activation is about forming a habit around the usage of a product, specifically deriving core value by fixing vulnerabilities. This definition is crucial because security is a team effort, and different team members may fulfill different parts of the activation journey.

To apply this insight to B2B products, consider defining activation milestones for teams based on aggregate level activity. This approach acknowledges that teams may not always be fully formed or active at the same time. By setting a milestone related to fixing vulnerabilities within a specific timeframe, you can encourage teams to establish a habit around using your product.

**Suggested application**

* Identify the key activities or milestones that indicate team activation, such as fixing vulnerabilities or completing a specific task.
* Set a reasonable timeframe for achieving these milestones, taking into account the typical onboarding process for your B2B product.
* Consider defining multiple milestones or stages of activation, allowing teams to progress at their own pace.
* Use these milestones to trigger targeted marketing campaigns, support, or resources to help teams get started and stay engaged with your product.

## Step 3: Focusing on Existing User Retention for B2B Products [3]

Albert Cheng highlights the importance of existing user retention for B2B products, particularly those with daily frequency. He notes that retention is crucial for building a habitual pattern and compounds over time.

According to Cheng, companies often focus on existing user retention mechanics, which can be a more effective lever than attracting new users.

### Suggested Application

To apply this principle to your B2B product, consider the following steps:

* Identify your most active users and understand their behavior and pain points.
* Develop a retention strategy that focuses on improving the user experience and reducing friction.
* Monitor and analyze user retention metrics to identify areas for improvement.
* Prioritize feature development and optimization based on user retention data.
* Continuously gather feedback from users to refine your retention strategy.

## Step 6: Establishing a Clear Activation Metric for B2B Products [1]

In establishing an activation metric, it's also important to consider the bar that needs to be set. A high bar can be beneficial, as it requires users to contribute meaningfully to the product, which can lead to a more engaged and retained user base. [1]

### Suggested Application

When establishing an activation metric for your B2B product, consider the following:

* Define a metric that requires users to create substance, such as a project or a workflow.
* Set a bar that requires users to collaborate on a regular cadence, such as weekly.
* Consider the type of contribution that is required, such as the number of users or the level of engagement.
* Ensure that the metric is measurable and can be tracked over time.

## Step 7: Defining Activation Milestones for B2B Products [2]

When defining activation milestones for B2B products, it's essential to consider the team's role in forming a habit around usage. According to Ben Williams from Snyk, activation is indicative of a team forming a habit around fixing vulnerabilities, not just logging in or finding vulnerabilities (Ben Williams [2]). This highlights the importance of measuring team-level activity, rather than individual user activity.

To apply this lesson to your B2B product, consider the following:

* **Identify key team milestones**: Determine what specific milestones indicate a team is forming a habit around using your product. This could be related to fixing vulnerabilities, completing onboarding, or achieving a specific business outcome.
* **Use aggregate level activity**: Measure aggregate level activity around these milestones, such as the number of vulnerabilities fixed or business outcomes achieved. This can help you identify teams that are actively using your product.
* **Set clear expectations**: Establish clear expectations for what constitutes an activation milestone, and communicate these to your teams and stakeholders.
* **Monitor and adjust**: Continuously monitor your teams' progress and adjust your activation milestones as needed to ensure they remain relevant and effective.
